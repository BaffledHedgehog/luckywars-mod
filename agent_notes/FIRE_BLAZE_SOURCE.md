## Item identity, donor and recipe

The normal `fallingstars` custom-data flag is the ordinary Blaze Wand donor: `transformables_staff.json` presents it as `lbc:blazewand` / `blaze_wand`; `lbcr/raycast.mcfunction:6` and `raycast_left.mcfunction:6` route it to the Blaze raycast for main/offhand. This is the exact `fallingstars` item used by the Fireblaze recipe, alongside `stfire`; the two recipe checks and consumers are `lbc/function/other/craft_ground.mcfunction:22`, `lbc/function/other/craft/inventory_craft_check.mcfunction:22`, `lbc/function/other/sauvojen_tuntija/crafted_blazefirewand.mcfunction:1-6`, and `crafted_blazefirewand_inv.mcfunction:1-12`. The result loot table at `lbc/loot_table/tuntijaa_fire_blaze.json:7-79` is one carrot-on-a-stick, model `lbc:blazefire`, tier A, named Fireblaze Wand, tagged `{transformable:1,staff:1,magicwand:1,fireblaze_wand:1}`. Do not substitute `truefallingstars`: it is a separate donor/flag with a different cast and price.

Both wand paths enter from `RC_Raycast` via `lbc/function/players_tick.mcfunction:19`, `lbc/function/items/spells.mcfunction:5,7,11,13`, and the default/upgraded dispatchers `lbc/function/workingitems/spell1_default.mcfunction:28`, `spell1_default_left.mcfunction:52`, `spell1_upgraded.mcfunction:33`, and `spell1_upgraded_left.mcfunction:29`. The configuration sets ordinary Blaze Wand cost to 2800 (`lbc/function/config.mcfunction:32`); Fireblaze Wand use costs 50 (`:90`). The ordinary wand's ray advances one block at a time up to 200 and stops on target/block/anti-magic conditions (`lbcr/function/raycast_loop4.mcfunction:1-5`), then creates a marker which fires small fireballs periodically (`lbcr/function/raycast_end4.mcfunction:1-7`, `lbc/function/tick1s.mcfunction:3`, `lbc/function/raycasters_1s.mcfunction:1,14`, `lbc/function/other/spawnfireball.mcfunction:1-4`). Those raycast fireballs have no owner NBT in this source. On a nearby Blaze (within 5 blocks of the endpoint), `podchinenie_blaze` moves it to the player's scoreboard team, copies caster ID/team scores to nearby Blazes, and refunds the full 2800; endpoint handling then continues and still creates its spell marker (`lbcr/function/raycast_end4_blaze_nyam.mcfunction:1-3`, `lbcr/function/podchinenie_blaze.mcfunction:1-36`, then `raycast_end4.mcfunction:3-7`). This is scoreboard/team conversion, not vanilla tame state.

Ordinary Fireblaze use calls `click2`, which launches one random-direction small fireball per eligible mob within 30 blocks, then deducts 50 mana (`lbc/function/workingitems/awaken_tuntija_wands/fireblaze/click.mcfunction:1-7`, `click2.mcfunction:1-3`, `at_random_fireball.mcfunction:1-14`). It excludes spectators, caster, same team and same ID. The spawned projectile receives the caster's ID/team scoreboards but has no explicit `Owner` field. When the player is sneaking with an `upgradable` Fireblaze Wand, `click2` additionally calls `click2_upgrade`: 14 fireballs are summoned at offsets reaching 150 blocks, with ExplosionPower 1–14, then the shared upgrade-ready callback is called (`click2.mcfunction:2`, `click2_upgrade.mcfunction:1-15`). This is not a tame/ally flow.

## Academy registration and flags: trace the whole chain

Academy interaction first distinguishes `upgraded`, `upgrade_available`, and `upgradable` (`lbc/function/other/magic_academy/interacted.mcfunction:3-8`). For a fresh Fireblaze Wand, `interacted_2.mcfunction:1-3` sets `upgradable:1`, and its Fireblaze branch at `:33` only prints `upgrade_condition_fireblaze` (`can_upgrade/fireblaze_wand.mcfunction:1`). The wand's sneaking trial (`click2_upgrade.mcfunction:1-15`) calls `lbc:other/magic_academy/can_upgrade`; generic `can_upgrade` delegates to `can_upgrade_book`, which sets `upgrade_available:1` and clears `upgradable` (`can_upgrade.mcfunction:1`, `can_upgrade_book.mcfunction:1-6`). Returning to Academy with that flag reaches `interacted_upgrade.mcfunction:5` and the Fireblaze branch at `:36`.

Crucially, Fireblaze's upgrade is not merely tier S: `upgrade/fireblaze_wand.mcfunction:1-2` applies `lbc:tiers/s`, then writes `upgraded:1` and `mode:1` through a temporary block item and adds the upgrade description (`interacted_upgrade.mcfunction:1-4`). This is the actual entry point for the upgraded callbacks. `lbc/advancement/true_advancements/legends/academy/blazefire.json:35-44` is separately an inventory achievement for the upgraded item, parented to the Fire Academy advancement; it is not the unlock check itself. `tuntija_wands/blazefire.json:35-44` is the corresponding ordinary crafted-item achievement.

The ordinary fallingstars/Blaze Wand has a separate Academy registration. `interacted_2.mcfunction:38` dispatches `can_upgrade/fallingstars`, which only shows the condition message; upgrade dispatch is `interacted_upgrade.mcfunction:41` and `upgrade/fallingstars.mcfunction:1-2` applies tier A. The shared upgrade function writes `upgraded:1` and `mode:1` before this branch, just as for other items. `academy/blaze.json` recognizes `{upgraded:1,transformable:1,staff:1,fallingstars:1}` and is parented below the Academy prism advancement (`lbc/advancement/true_advancements/legends/academy/blaze.json:18-44`).

## Fallingstars Academy trial and post-upgrade modes

The ordinary fallingstars wand's first raycast function always performs the normal ray, then, only while sneaking and holding a wand with `upgradable:1,fallingstars:1`, calls `lbcr:raycast_loop4cd_upgrade` (`lbcr/function/raycast_loop4cd.mcfunction:1-5`). That function issues twenty bare `summon minecraft:blaze` commands and calls the shared `can_upgrade` callback (`lbcr/function/raycast_loop4cd_upgrade.mcfunction:1-21`). These are untagged default Blazes: this function assigns no owner/team, does not tame or remove them, and contains no refund. The callback immediately sets `upgrade_available:1` and clears `upgradable:1`; there is no further combat, damage, kill, or timing threshold (`lbc/function/other/magic_academy/can_upgrade.mcfunction:1`, `can_upgrade_book.mcfunction:1-6`). It is a spell-triggered Academy trial/upgrade-ready flag, not a separate Fireblaze recipe or separate Academy result. The source does not explain whether the unowned Blazes are meant as a permanent wave, an Academy test, or a cleanup omission.

After upgrading, fallingstars uses `raycast_loop4cd_upgraded.mcfunction` and `raycast_loop4cd_upgraded2.mcfunction`: mode 1 runs the standard ray, mode 2 invokes `raycast_loop4cd_upgraded2_blaze_head`; each actual cast deducts the full 2800 (`raycast_loop4cd_upgraded2.mcfunction:1-5`). Mode changes happen while sneaking using Academy `change_mode_2`; the UI names mode 2 `mode_blaze_head` (`raycast_loop4cd_upgraded.mcfunction:1-3`; offhand counterparts in `raycast_loop4cd_upgraded_left.mcfunction:1-8`). Mode 2 itself adds 7 to the player's `blaze` objective and plays a sound (`raycast_loop4cd_upgraded2_blaze_head.mcfunction:1-2`).

That score is a global player resource, not a wand-local buff: `players_10t.mcfunction:2` runs `other/blazehead` every 10 ticks whenever `blaze>=1`, unless a `stopper_magic` marker is within 20. Each pulse shoots once, subtracts 1, gives 2 seconds of fire resistance, and plays a sound (`other/blazehead.mcfunction:1-4`). Its helper emits a small fireball, explicitly writes the player's UUID to projectile `Owner`, faces the nearest non-spectator/non-caster/non-map-object mob, then removes the temporary caster tag (`other/blazehead1.mcfunction:1-6`). Thus a +7 cast schedules up to seven autonomous shots over about 3.5 seconds, gated by nearby anti-magic; it does not charge mana again per pulse. Other sources can modify `blaze` too (`kfc_chick/all_up_scores_no_mana.mcfunction:86` adds one; `netherite_stew.mcfunction:35` sets it to max int), so the aura is shared with those effects. The ordinary upgraded Blaze Wand trial uses the twenty-Blaze callback above; it is separate from this mode-2 aura.

## Fireblaze upgraded modes and cast price

An upgraded Fireblaze cast still costs 50 mana: `spell1_upgraded*.mcfunction` dispatches it, and `click_upgraded_pre.mcfunction:1-6` executes the chosen mode, then subtracts `fireblaze_wand_mana_use`. Sneak changes one of four modes through `change_mode_4` / `_left`; names and default mode handling are in `click_upgraded.mcfunction:1-7` and `click_upgraded_left.mcfunction:1-8`. Mode is stored on the held item and loaded by `click2_upgraded.mcfunction:1-9`.

- **World (mode 1):** one random-direction small fireball per eligible mob in the loaded entity selector, with no range bound (`click2_upgraded_mode_world.mcfunction:1-2`; projectile patterns/score ownership at `at_random_fireball.mcfunction:1-14`).
- **Mega (mode 2):** a random-direction fireball with ExplosionPower 5 per eligible mob within 100 blocks (`click2_upgraded_mode_mega.mcfunction:1-2`; `at_random_big_fireball.mcfunction:1-14`).
- **Pillars (mode 3):** place a marker at each eligible mob, then for each marker perform the shared `universal_pos_razb_30`, fill its X/Z column with lava from Y=0 through 319, and kill the marker (`click2_upgraded_mode_pillars.mcfunction:1-4`, `at_pillar.mcfunction:1`, `pillar.mcfunction:1-5`). The shared helper first moves each marker by random X/Z offsets from -30 to +30 (`lbc/function/universal_pos_razb_30.mcfunction:1-10`), so the columns are displaced from their mob targets. This is a world-scale destructive effect; target selection has no explicit range bound.
- **Kiuas (mode 4):** grants infinite fire resistance, applies the small Kiuaskivi helper to up to 100 eligible mobs, then the large helper to up to 10 (`click2_upgraded_mode_kiuas.mcfunction:1-3`). Small helper: ±1 X/Z, Y=0..1 relative to target, ordered air/fire fills. Large helper: ±15 cube air/fire, then a central lava cube ±1. `universal_pos_razb_30` belongs to Pillars, not this mode. Overlap between different targets follows that source-wide order.

Eligible Fireblaze targets are `#minecraft:mobs`, not spectator/caster, and fail same-team and same-ID predicates. Ordinary Fireblaze projectiles only receive score attribution, no explicit Owner; mega projectiles likewise set `lbcID2`/`team_number`, but the code has a spelling mismatch in their temp tag (`cusrpawn` on summon while later lookup expects `curspawn`), so assignment may fail (`at_random_big_fireball.mcfunction:2-14`). Pillar mode writes lava directly. Attribution, target filters, world bounds, and destructive pillar extent need native/port decisions based on the desired behavior, not assumptions that datapack tags imply a functioning projectile owner.

## Distinct truefallingstars donor

`truefallingstars` has separate mana configuration (4000, `lbc/function/config.mcfunction:30`) and `lbcr/raycast.mcfunction:7` routing. Its distinct ray end summons a descending fireball pattern with ExplosionPower 2/4 (`lbcr/raycast_loop5.mcfunction:1-5`, `raycast_end5.mcfunction:9-24`). The Fireblaze recipe input and normal Blaze Wand are `fallingstars`, not `truefallingstars`.

## Clarifications and port risks

- The twenty-Blaze fallingstars trial creates unowned default Blazes and never cleans them up or refunds the 2800 cast cost. Is that spawn intended to persist as a hostile trial wave, or should the port track/tame the Blazes and remove them after the trial? This is separate from ordinary raycast endpoint behavior, which team-converts a nearby Blaze and refunds 2800. Source has no “meditation” phase or trial threshold here: the 20 summons are followed immediately by setting `upgrade_available`.
- Blaze-head mode's seven-charge autonomous aura is a different feature from the twenty-Blaze Academy trial. Confirm whether the port should retain the aura's shared global `blaze` pool, 10-tick cadence, anti-magic pause, free mana pulses, and two-second fire resistance.
- Fireblaze pillar mode writes lava across Y=0..319 for every selected target, and has no explicit target range. Confirm this reach/world effect before migration.
- Normal Fireblaze and mega projectiles lack native owner attribution; mega's `cusrpawn`/`curspawn` mismatch likely prevents its ID/team copy. Preserve intended owner attribution in the port, not this source defect.
- `can_upgrade/fireblaze_wand` and `can_upgrade/fallingstars` are display-only messages; the actual `upgrade_available` flag is set by their cast/trial callback through shared `can_upgrade_book`. Do not model the Academy condition text as an implemented condition predicate.
